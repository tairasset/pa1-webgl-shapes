## E1 — Order of transformations

Baseline: M = Rx(2πt/14) · T(0,2.5,0) · Ry(2t) · S(s(t)).
glMatrix right-multiplies; a vertex receives scale first, then self-spin,
translation and orbit rotation. Copies below freeze the scene at t=3.5 s.

### Swap orbit rotation and translation

Prediction: T · Rx · Ry · S leaves the centre at (0,2.5,0), so orbital
movement disappears. The solid still changes orientation and scale.

Result: see baseline.png and swap-orbit-translation.png, and console-output.txt.

Explanation: rotating the local origin does not move it; the final translation
places it at one fixed point. In the baseline, Rx rotates the translated centre.

### Swap translation and self-spin

Prediction: Rx · Ry · T · S produces the same motion for this variant.
The translation (0,2.5,0) is parallel to the self-spin y-axis, so Ry and T commute.

Result: see swap-translation-self.png and the numerical comparison in console-output.txt.

Explanation: this is a special commuting pair, not evidence that matrix
multiplication generally commutes. A translation with an x or z component
would make the order affect the position.

## E2 — Three statements in Chapter 4

Prediction: [0,1,0] denotes y, not x. Removing geometry at frustum boundaries
is clipping; face culling discards faces according to winding/facing.
Translating a direction with w=0 should leave it unchanged; w=1 should move a point.

Result: for translation (2,3,4), vec4.transformMat4 returns
[1,0,0,0] and [3,3,4,1], respectively. See homogeneous-w.png and console-output.txt.
Rotation by 90 degrees about [0,1,0] sends [1,0,0,1] to approximately
[0,0,-1,1], showing that it rotates x/z while preserving y.

Explanation: the chapter's axis comment is incorrect. Its use of “culling”
confuses clipping with face culling. The fourth component of a position vec4
is a homogeneous coordinate, not a quaternion component. For affine transforms,
w=0 represents a direction and w=1 represents a point; translation contributes
its offset multiplied by w. Perspective subsequently divides clip xyz by clip w.
