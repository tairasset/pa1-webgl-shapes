## E3 — Perspective versus orthographic

Prediction: at the identical paused t=0 pose, perspective should make the
near vertical edge longer than the far edge. Orthographic lengths should match.

Method: the experiment projects endpoints of two original y-directed cube
edges, at x=0.5 and z=±0.5, through the actual P·V·M matrices into CSS pixels.
The near edge is orange. The far edge is hidden by opaque faces, so a cyan
screen-space guide shows its projected location. Measurements are projected
endpoint distances, rather than independent ruler measurements of raster pixels.

Result: see projection-perspective.png, projection-orthographic.png and E3-E4-output.txt.

At viewport 1280×720, FOV 45° and paused t=0:

| Projection | Near edge (CSS px) | Far edge (CSS px) | Near/far ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Perspective | 116.65 | 104.41 | 1.1172 |
| Orthographic | 110.11 | 110.11 | 1.0000 |

The tiny unrounded orthographic difference is floating-point error.

Explanation: perspective divides clip xyz by clip w, which varies with depth.
Orthographic w remains 1, so equal parallel world-space segments retain equal
projected lengths. The PA1 depth-offset trick resembles an oblique parallel
projection: depth adds a fixed x/y shift without distance-dependent shrinking.
It is closer to orthographic than perspective, but is not strictly orthographic.

## E4 — Near, far and aspect

Prediction: near=0.1 and far=20 contain the scene. Raising near to 5.2 should
cut the solid near its closest approach. Using aspect=1 in a wide viewport
should stretch the scene horizontally.

Result: compare near-reference.png with near-cut.png at t=3.5; compare
aspect-square.png, aspect-wide-correct.png and aspect-wide-one.png.
Exact viewport sizes and measurements are recorded in E3-E4-output.txt.
The near=5.2 screenshot shows a cut through the frustum at the same pose;
the near=0.1 reference shows its complete faces. At 720×720, aspect=1 is
correct. At 1440×720, the aspect=1 copy stretches horizontally, whereas
the correct aspect=2 copy preserves the shape. No browser console errors
were recorded during these experiments.

Explanation: the eye is √55.25≈7.433 units from the origin. All vertices lie
inside a radius of 2.5+0.8√3/2≈3.193 around the origin, a deliberately loose
bound. The eye-to-vertex distance and positive camera-axis depth are therefore
bounded conservatively by 4.24–10.63, for every pose. Near=0.1 and far=20
comfortably bracket these bounds. Numerical sampling gives tighter depths
around 4.63–10.23, but sampling alone is not a proof for every instant.
Clipping planes constrain view-axis depth rather than Euclidean radial distance.
With aspect=1, projection fails to compensate for the actual width/height;
at 1440×720, horizontal dimensions become twice their correct size.
